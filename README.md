## 🔍 What I’m Exploring

I'm teaching myself the foundations of machine learning and AI — because I have these big, weird, important questions I can’t stop thinking about. A lot of my learning right now is hands-on: building tiny models, running experiments, poking around under the hood. These are the questions pulling me forward:

- **How can we tell what a model *really* means when it says something?**  
  Not just statistically — but structurally. Is there a way to trace internal meaning?

- **What would a “Rosetta Stone” for AI look like?**  
  Can we translate the math back into something human-readable?

- **How do we know when a model is *actually aligned* — not just saying what it thinks we want to hear?**  
  What does safety-shaped performance hide?

- **What happens if we train a model inside a world we already understand?**  
  Could we observe it learning in a labeled, structured environment — and finally *see* how internal representations form?

- **What does it mean when a model hallucinates?**  
  Can hallucinations be useful maps of internal token relationships?

- **How do models assign confidence? Can they know when they don’t know?**

- **Can we build tiny transparent models as “lab rats” — to dissect and watch misalignment as it develops?**

- **What could humor or surprise teach us about how models make connections?**

- **And finally… who aligns the aligners?**  
  What happens when we build models to evaluate the safety of other models? Where does the recursion stop?
